PEAK250 recurring precharge-module fault

A station-level hardware fault — HCU Precharge Module Fault_0x27 (vendor code 012900) — aborted three consecutive customer charging sessions within 41 minutes on 2026-07-26, each while power was already flowing. Service was restored by a remote hard reset, but the fault is a degrading hardware condition that recurs on charging and requires vendor inspection.

01

Summary

Times are shown in UTC with site-local CEST (UTC+2) in parentheses; the charger reports in UTC.

  • The dual-gun battery-buffered PEAK250 raised the identical fault three times — 09:14:47, 09:27:27 and 09:55:08 UTC (11:14, 11:27, 11:55 CEST).
  • Each occurrence was a station-level fault (OCPP connector 0) that forced both guns to Faulted and aborted the active Gun 1 (CCS) session.
  • All three happened during charging at 52–75 kW, not at plug-in, and under normal operating conditions (no overload).
  • The time-to-fault shortened on each attempt — 2 min 40 s → 40 s → 30 s — the signature of a degrading component.
  • A separate, isolated SECC sequence error (vendor 163200) occurred earlier at 06:06 UTC on Gun 2 and did not recur.

03

Root-cause analysis

Four independent observations point to a defective, degrading component on the shared DC power stage.

SHARED

It downs both guns → shared component

The fault is reported on OCPP connector 0 (the station itself) and forces both charging points to Faulted. In the battery-buffered architecture both guns draw from one shared DC/power stage — consistent with the precharge module sitting on that shared bus rather than on a single gun.

MID-SESSION

It fires during charging, not at plug-in

Precharge normally runs once at session start to match the DC link to the EV battery before closing the main contactor. Here it faulted 2.5 minutes into a session with 62 kW already flowing — an unusual timing that suggests either continuous precharge-circuit monitoring detecting a fault, or a mid-session precharge re-run (e.g. on buffer↔grid source switching).

NORMAL LOAD

It occurs under normal conditions

At fault time the car drew 62–75 kW, buffer SoC was 94–100%, and grid infeed was ~40 kW — well within rating. Nothing was overloaded, which argues for a hardware defect rather than an operating-condition trip.

DEGRADING

Onset accelerates on each attempt

Time-to-fault fell from 2 min 40 s to 40 s to 30 s across the three attempts. A component that fails progressively sooner is the classic signature of thermal or wear-driven degradation, not a one-off transient.

Working hypothesis: a failing precharge module (contactor / resistor / DC-link monitoring) on the shared DC stage, degrading under repeated load. The precise meaning of internal sub-code 0x27 and vendor code 012900 requires ZD's confirmation.

04

Customer impact

Three charging attempts were aborted mid-session. Signed OCMF meter data exists for each, so billing is defensible.

  • tx 1021 — 2.37 kWh delivered before abort; receipt #00000368.
  • tx 1022 — 0.20 kWh delivered (same driver retried); receipt #00000370.
  • tx 1023 — a new customer, ~30 s of charging before abort.

Each session ended with OCPP StopTransaction reason Other (fault-terminated), not Local (customer-ended). Affected drivers should be refunded to the actual energy delivered, and the pre-authorization holds released.

05

Response & remediation

DONE

Remote hard reset — service restored

A hard reset was issued from our backend at 09:59:43 UTC and accepted by the charger. It power-cycled and returned with both guns Available and no fault state at 10:04 UTC — ~5 minutes total. A soft reset was deliberately avoided, as it would restart only the application and not clear a latched hardware fault.

DONE

Remote take-out-of-service capability added

The incident exposed that we had no way to remotely fence off faulty hardware. We wired the OCPP ChangeAvailability operation into the backend command surface (POST /chargers/{id}/commands/change-availability, Operative / Inoperative), so a faulting unit can now be marked Inoperative remotely pending inspection.

ACTION

Recommended: take the unit out of service

The reset clears the fault state but not the cause; the fault recurs on charging. Until ZD inspects, the unit (or at minimum Gun 1) should be set Inoperative so arriving customers see "unavailable" rather than a failed charge and a pre-auth hold.

Questions for ZD:

  • What does 012900 / HCU Prechrg Module Fault_0x27 indicate specifically — which component (precharge contactor, resistor, DC-link monitoring)?
  • Why would a precharge fault trigger minutes into an active session, long after precharge completes? Is precharge re-run mid-session (e.g. on buffer↔grid source switching) in the buffered design?
  • Why does the fault take both charging points down — is the precharge module shared across the two DC outputs?
  • Does the accelerating onset (2 m 40 s → 40 s → 30 s) indicate a degrading / thermal condition versus a hard failure? Is the unit safe to keep in service?
  • Recommended action — inspection or part replacement?
  • Separately: is 163200 (SECC sequence error) on Gun 2 related, or an independent vehicle-handshake issue?
